\section*{S3. Theorem cards for formal results}

For each of the six formal results summarised in Section~2.1 and Table~2 of the
main text, this supplement lists: the source and version consulted, the model class,
data regime, loss function, asymptotic variable, collapse criterion and main
assumptions, and the principal ``does not imply'' caveat stated in the main text.

\paragraph{Result 1: full replacement leads to generation-wise error growth.}
\begin{description}
\item[Sources]
  Dohmatob et al.\ (2024a), Theorem~1 of arXiv:2402.07043 (original result);
  Gerstgrasser et al.\ COLM 2024 (arXiv:2404.01413, Eq.~(4)) restates it to
  contrast with the accumulation bound.
\item[Model class] Ordinary least squares (Gaussian linear regression).
\item[Regime] Replace (no real data after generation~0); equal sample size $N$ at
  each generation.
\item[Loss / asymptotic variable] Quadratic loss; asymptotic in generations $g$.
\item[Design] Fixed initial real dataset $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$; same
  design matrix used for synthetic target generation at every generation.
\item[Assumptions] $N > d+1$; isotropic design $\Sigma = I_d$; independent noise
  $\sigma^2$ per label.
\item[Main result] $E_{\text{test}}^{\text{Replace}}(\hat w_g) = E_0 \cdot g$,
  where $E_0 = \sigma^2 d / (N - d - 1)$.
\item[Does not imply] Error growth when real data are retained (Result~2); or that
  neural scaling laws are invalid for frontier LLMs trained on mixed corpora.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Result 2: accumulation bounds the error.}
\begin{description}
\item[Sources]
  Gerstgrasser et al.\ COLM 2024 (arXiv:2404.01413, Theorem~1); empirical
  support across architectures and datasets in Kazdan et al.\ ICML 2025
  (arXiv:2410.16713).
\item[Model class] As Result~1.
\item[Regime] Accumulate (original real data retained and accumulated alongside each
  new generation of synthetic data).
\item[Design] Fixed initial real dataset (same $X$ accumulated at every generation).
\item[Main result]
  $E_g = E_0 \sum_{i=1}^{g+1} i^{-2} < E_0 \pi^2/6 \approx 1.645\,E_0$.
\item[Numerical check] Supplementary Material~S4 confirms the equality form holds
  under the fixed design and shows the bound is not attained with fresh inputs.
\item[Does not imply] Bounded error under temperature-biased sampling (Result~6);
  or that the synthetic fraction is bounded (it can approach~1 under accumulation
  while the bound still holds).
\end{description}

\paragraph{Result 3: a fixed synthetic fraction produces an error floor.}
\begin{description}
\item[Source] Dohmatob et al.\ ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2405.05846), Theorem~3.2.
\item[Model class] High-dimensional linear regression (single training run,
  non-recursive); covariate distribution non-isotropic.
\item[Regime] Fixed-mix: a fraction $p \in (0,1)$ of training data is synthetic in
  a single run.
\item[Loss / asymptotic variable] Quadratic loss; asymptotic in training set size
  $N$ (not in generations).
\item[Main result] Non-vanishing excess error floor
  $\Delta(p) > 0$ determined by $p$ and the gap between real and synthetic
  distributions; floor $\to 0$ as $p \to 0$.
\item[Does not imply] That this floor applies in multi-generation recursive training
  (different model from Results~1--2); or that neural scaling laws are invalid for
  frontier LLMs.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Result 4: without real data, the generative distribution degenerates.}
\begin{description}
\item[Sources]
  Shumailov et al.\ Nature 2024 (original qualitative result, Gaussian mixture
  and language model experiments);
  Seddik et al.\ COLM 2024 (arXiv:2404.01413-bis, Theorem~1), statistical LM:
  model collapse cannot be avoided under fully synthetic training; probability mass
  concentrates and tails disappear;
  Suresh et al.\ 2025 (arXiv:2412.17646, Theorem~1): for discrete distributions,
  the expected number of symbols surviving after $k$ iterations is
  $\sum_i \exp(-n p_i g_k)$, where $p_i$ is the original frequency; survival time
  $\approx n p_i$ generations.
\item[Model class] Language / discrete / Gaussian models; fully synthetic regime.
\item[Regime] Replace (fully synthetic; no real data after generation~0).
\item[Loss] Maximum likelihood / cross-entropy.
\item[Main result] Tails of the original distribution disappear; symbols survive
  for approximately $n \cdot p_i$ generations (Theorem~1 of Suresh et al.); full
  degeneration to a limited vocabulary or singular distribution under repeated
  replacement (Seddik et al., Shumailov et al.).
\item[Does not imply] That collapse is unavoidable with any real data (see
  Results~2--3); or a specific rate for LLMs trained on web-scale corpora.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Result 5: a random-walk view of parameter drift.}
\begin{description}
\item[Source] Xu et al.\ arXiv:2505.13947v1 (2025).
  Theorems used: Theorem~1 (divergence under constant $n$, Gaussian variance
  estimation); Theorem~3 (sufficiency of $c_t = t^{1+s}$ for unbiased estimators
  with $\kappa \ge \gamma/2$); Theorem~5 ($P(T) < 1/2$ for any schedule under
  asymptotic normality).
\item[Model class] Generic exponential family under uniform tail-bound
  Assumption~1; unbiasedness required for Theorem~3 (Assumption~2).
\item[Regime] Fully synthetic; varying sample schedule $n_t = c_t \cdot n$.
\item[Asymptotic variable] Number of recursive training steps $T$; limit taken
  jointly as $n \to \infty$ and $T \to \infty$.
\item[Main results]
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Theorem~1} (Gaussian variance, constant $n$):
    $E[(\hat\sigma^2_T - \sigma^2)^2] \to \infty$ as $T \to \infty$; diversity
    vanishes with probability~1.
  \item \emph{Theorem~3} (general unbiased, $\kappa \ge \gamma/2$):
    schedule $c_t = t^{1+s}$ ($s > 0$) satisfies
    $\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{T\to\infty} P(\|\hat\theta_T - \theta^*\|_2 \ge \delta)
    = 0$ for any $\delta > 0$.
  \item \emph{Theorem~5} ($P(T)$ bound): under asymptotic normality, the probability
    that the $T$-th synthetic-data estimate outperforms the real-data estimate is
    $< \tfrac{1}{2}$ for any $T$ and any sample schedule.
  \end{itemize}
\item[Does not imply] Negative expected return on synthetic training (Theorem~5
  bounds probability, not expectation); coexistence of bounded error and useful
  synthetic training illustrated in S4.2; the $c_t = t^{1+s}$ schedule is
  sufficient (Theorem~3), not necessary.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Result 6: temperature-filtered sampling under accumulation.}
\begin{description}
\item[Source] Marchi et al.\ IEEE CDC 2024 (DOI: 10.1109/CDC56724.2024.10886816),
  Theorems~1--3.
\item[Model class] Discrete generative model with finite output set; closed-loop
  maximum likelihood or cross-entropy; exact-tracking limit ($\delta = 0$ per-step
  approximation error).
\item[Regime] Accumulate; original real data retained.
\item[Asymptotic variable] Number of generated samples (asymptotic in size of
  synthetic batches per generation, not in number of generations).
\item[Main results]
  \begin{itemize}
  \item $T_s = 1$ (neutral sampling): dynamics converge to a finite drift; the
    accumulation bound of Result~2 holds.
  \item $T_s \neq 1$ (temperature-biased sampling): distribution converges to
    the uniform distribution on a subset of the vocabulary, or to the simplex
    boundary, depending on $T_s$ and the initial distribution.
  \end{itemize}
\item[Does not imply] That accumulation fails generally; only that it fails when
  sampling is temperature-biased. The closed-loop approximation ($\delta = 0$) is an
  idealisation not achieved in practice.
\end{description}
